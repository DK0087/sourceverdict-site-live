Real verdict · Wooden watch

We ran the investigation on a wooden watch.

Product
A printed wooden wristwatch, sourced from an overseas marketplace listing. Target markets: US + Canada.
What looked attractive
Eye-catching print, obvious gift potential, visible customization, and an approachable-looking order minimum.
What we found
The same image already appears across Alibaba and AliExpress — a striking design isn't proof of exclusivity. Comparable-watch buyers reported loose hands, broken straps and a detached crystal. Supplier-price currency and full landed cost were unconfirmed, with a 50-piece customization minimum shown. Different models; individual reports, not measured defect rates.
Verdict
GO — for a small custom pilot. Negotiate the MOQ, approve samples, and validate costs before committing inventory.

Signature method

Bad reviews are product intelligence.

The one-star reviews on competing products are a map. Each complaint is an unmet need — and an unmet need is the brief for a better product.

Customer complaint

"The strap broke twice — and I couldn't get replacement parts."

→
Unmet need

Repairability. Buyers want a product that survives ownership — and a way to fix it when it doesn't.

→
Better product idea

Replaceable links with a part code, available spares, and a simple sizing guide in the box.

Complaints are paraphrased individual reports about comparable products — treated as leads to investigate, not verified defect rates.
