How we handle evidence

Every claim is labelled. Nothing is dressed up.

Investigative discipline is the whole point. We separate what's proven from what's estimated from what's still unknown — so you can trust the GO or PASS.

Verified fact

Observed directly in a screenshot or an accessible public page — with its limits stated.

Estimate

A calculated figure with its assumptions attached. Labelled, never presented as certainty.

Assumption

A working hypothesis we think is reasonable — and flag as a thing to test.

Unknown

What we couldn't confirm yet, and what it would take to resolve it.

We don't fabricate sales data, invent testimonials, or turn a seller's description into tested performance. If the evidence is thin, the report says so.

The verdict

What GO and PASS actually mean.

GO

Worth exploring further.

The evidence supports a next step — often a sample or a small pilot, with named conditions to confirm. A GO is not a purchase order or a promise of profit, and a GO for a pilot is not a GO for inventory.

PASS

Looks tempting, but not strong enough.

On the current evidence, this version isn't worth your inventory. A PASS isn't "this can never sell" — it's what would need to change, and why. A supported PASS is a valid, useful outcome.

Data & sources

Public evidence first — licensed data where warranted.

Investigations start from observable public evidence: marketplace listings, retail pricing, reviews and supplier pages, each dated and linked. In a Full Decision Report, licensed Amazon data is used only where it's available and permitted — and we tell you when a number comes from it. We don't claim data we didn't actually access.

Important

Decision support — not a guarantee of profit.

SourceVerdict reduces uncertainty before you commit money. It does not guarantee sales, margins, supplier performance or regulatory approval. You stay in control of the decision; we make sure it's an informed one. Products with specialised safety, medical, food or electrical requirements may need a different scope — tell us up front.
